## The Short Answer

A professional website in Singapore costs between $3,000 and $30,000+, depending on complexity. But the real question isn't "how much" -- it's "what are you actually paying for?"

This guide breaks down what drives website costs, what you should expect at each price point, and how to avoid overpaying.

## Website Cost Ranges in Singapore

| **Type** | **Price Range** | **Timeline** | **Best For** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Landing page (1-5 pages) | $2,000 - $5,000 | 2-4 weeks | New businesses, campaigns, MVPs |
| Business website (5-15 pages) | $5,000 - $15,000 | 4-8 weeks | SMBs, professional services, F&B |
| E-commerce site | $8,000 - $25,000 | 6-12 weeks | Retail, D2C brands |
| Custom web application | $15,000 - $50,000+ | 8-16 weeks | SaaS, platforms, enterprise tools |

## What Actually Drives the Cost

### 1. Design Complexity

A templated design is cheaper but looks like everyone else. Custom design costs more but gives your brand a distinct identity. For most Singapore SMBs, a semi-custom approach (custom layout, standard components) hits the sweet spot.

### 2. Content Management

Do you need to update content yourself? A proper CMS (content management system) adds development time but saves you money long-term. You should never have to call your developer just to update a phone number.

### 3. Integrations

Payment gateways (Stripe, PayNow), booking systems, CRM connections, WhatsApp chat widgets -- each integration adds complexity and cost. List your must-haves upfront so there are no surprises.

### 4. SEO Foundation

A cheap website without SEO is invisible. Proper technical SEO, meta tags, page speed optimisation, and mobile responsiveness should be included in the base price, not sold as an add-on.

### 5. Hosting & Maintenance

Many agencies quote low upfront costs then lock you into expensive hosting contracts. Ask about ongoing costs before signing anything.

## Red Flags When Getting Quotes

- **"$500 for a full website"** -- you're getting a template with your logo slapped on. No strategy, no SEO, no custom design.
- **No clear scope document** -- if they can't tell you exactly what's included, you'll face scope creep and surprise invoices.
- **They don't ask about your business goals** -- a website isn't just pixels. It should be built around what you're trying to achieve.
- **No timeline or milestones** -- professional agencies work in phases with clear deliverables at each stage.
- **They can't show similar work** -- ask for case studies or live sites, not just mockups.

## What to Expect from a Professional Agency

A good Singapore web development agency should provide:

1. **Discovery phase** -- understanding your business, audience, and goals before writing a single line of code
2. **Detailed proposal** -- clear scope, timeline, deliverables, and payment schedule
3. **Design review rounds** -- you see and approve the design before development starts
4. **Mobile-first development** -- over 70% of Singapore web traffic is mobile
5. **Testing & QA** -- thorough testing across devices and browsers
6. **Launch support** -- DNS setup, SSL certificates, analytics configuration
7. **Post-launch warranty** -- a period where bugs are fixed at no extra cost

## The WordPress Question

WordPress powers roughly 40% of the web, and it's the default choice for many agencies. It works well for blogs and simple sites, but it comes with trade-offs:

- **Security maintenance** -- WordPress sites need constant plugin updates to stay secure
- **Performance overhead** -- plugins slow your site down over time
- **Hosting costs** -- managed WordPress hosting in Singapore runs $30-100/month

Modern alternatives like Next.js with a headless CMS can deliver faster, more secure sites with lower long-term hosting costs. Worth asking your agency about.

## How to Get the Best Value

1. **Define your goals first** -- "I need a website" is not a brief. "I need a site that generates 20 enquiries per month from Singapore SMB owners" is.
2. **Get 3 quotes** -- but compare scope, not just price. The cheapest quote often means the most corners cut.
3. **Ask about the tech stack** -- what they build with affects your long-term costs and flexibility.
4. **Check ongoing costs** -- hosting, maintenance, SSL, domain renewal. Get the full picture.
5. **Own your code** -- make sure the contract gives you ownership of the final product.
