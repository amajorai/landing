Wix is great - until it isn't. And knowing when you've crossed that line can save your business months of frustration and thousands of dollars in migration costs.

This isn't a "Wix bad, custom good" post. Both have their place. The real question is which one fits where your business is right now - and where it's headed.

## When Wix actually makes sense

Let's be fair. Wix (and Squarespace, and similar builders) are genuinely good for:

- **Brand new businesses** testing an idea before committing real budget
- **Solo operators** who need a simple online presence - a few pages, contact form, maybe a blog
- **Very tight budgets** (under $2,000) where any professional site is better than no site
- **Temporary projects** - event pages, campaign microsites, pop-up shops

If you're a freelance photographer who needs a portfolio and a contact page, Wix at $20/month is perfectly sensible. No one should tell you otherwise.

## The 7 signs you've outgrown a website builder

### 1. Your site looks like everyone else's

Wix templates are polished, but they're shared across millions of sites. When your competitors' websites feel interchangeable with yours, you have a branding problem that no template tweak can fix.

### 2. You're fighting the platform instead of using it

Spending hours trying to make a section behave differently, or installing plugin after plugin to get basic functionality? That's the platform's limits talking.

### 3. Your site is slow and you can't fix it

Page builders load a lot of code you don't need. A Wix site that takes 4-5 seconds to load is losing visitors - and Google notices. You can't optimise what you don't control.

### 4. You need real integrations

Connecting to your CRM, syncing inventory with your POS system, building a customer portal - these aren't things website builders handle well. You end up with Zapier duct-tape solutions that break silently.

### 5. SEO has hit a ceiling

Basic SEO works fine on Wix. Advanced SEO - custom schema markup, server-side rendering, fine-tuned Core Web Vitals, programmatic pages - requires control over your codebase.

### 6. You can't comply with regulations

PDPA compliance, WCAG accessibility standards, specific data handling requirements - if your industry has compliance needs, you need a site you can fully audit and control.

### 7. Your business depends on the website

If the website IS the business (e-commerce, SaaS, marketplace, booking platform), building on someone else's platform means your core asset sits on rented land.

## The real cost comparison

|  | **Wix/Squarespace** | **Custom website** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Upfront cost** | $0 - $500 | $5,000 - $30,000+ |
| **Monthly cost** | $20 - $50/month | $20 - $100/month (hosting) |
| **3-year total** | $720 - $2,300 | $5,720 - $33,600 |
| **Performance** | Limited by platform | Fully optimisable |
| **Ownership** | Platform-dependent | You own everything |

The upfront cost difference is real. But consider: if your website generates even one extra lead per month because it loads faster, ranks higher, and converts better - how quickly does that pay for itself?

## The hidden costs of staying on a builder

- **Plugin fees** that stack up ($10-50/month each for forms, booking, analytics, SEO tools)
- **Migration costs** when you eventually outgrow it (often more expensive than building custom from the start)
- **Lost revenue** from slower load times and limited functionality
- **Developer frustration** - finding someone to fix niche Wix issues is harder and pricier than you'd expect

## What does "custom" actually mean in 2026?

Custom doesn't mean coding from scratch in a dark room. Modern custom websites use frameworks and tools that make development faster than ever:

- **Next.js** for blazing-fast, SEO-friendly sites
- **Headless CMS** (Sanity, Strapi, Payload) so you can edit content yourself without touching code
- **Component libraries** that speed up development while keeping things unique
- **Modern hosting** (Coolify, Vercel, VPS) that's cheap and reliable

A good agency builds you a site that's custom in the ways that matter (design, performance, functionality) and efficient where it doesn't (content management, deployment).

## The smart migration path

If you're on Wix and thinking about switching, here's a sensible approach:

1. **Audit what's working.** Keep the content and structure that performs well.
2. **Define what's broken.** What specifically can't you do on Wix?
3. **Scope the MVP.** Migrate with core features first, then add enhancements.
4. **Set up redirects.** Preserve your SEO equity - every URL should redirect properly.
5. **Plan the content transition.** Don't lose blog posts, images, or metadata in the move.
